WEEK01ARRIVAL

LECTURES · WELCOME WEEK · FINDING MY BEARINGS

Arriving, Listening, Exploring

Weekly journal

My first week at the University of Leeds was shaped by lectures, welcome meetings and the beginning of a new routine. There were no workshops yet. Instead, the week gave me time to listen, observe and find my bearings.

Coming from China to study for a postgraduate degree in Leeds feels very different from my previous experience of student life. Even familiar things—attending a lecture, meeting people and finding my way around—seem new in this setting. I am still learning how this place works and where I might fit into it.

Welcome Week also introduced me to opportunities beyond the classroom. I am curious about societies and events such as Give It a Go, where students can try activities without committing straight away. For now, I am exploring what is available and looking forward to taking part.

Weekly reflection

This week feels more like a beginning than a week of making. I am adjusting to a different academic and social environment, and trying to stay open to what I have not experienced yet.

I am especially curious about the workshops. Lectures have given me an introduction to the course, but I am looking forward to learning through practical activities and seeing how ideas develop into work. I do not know exactly what to expect yet, and that uncertainty is part of this first stage: noticing, exploring and gradually making a place for myself here.

WEEK02FIRST BUILD

WORKSHOP · HTML & CSS · GITHUB PAGES

Choosing a Platform, Learning by Making

Weekly journal

This week, we began our workshops and started creating our portfolio websites. We were introduced to two possible platforms: CampusPress and GitHub Pages. I chose GitHub Pages, where I can build my site using HTML and CSS. The choice feels closely connected to the kind of personal digital world I want to create, and GitHub Pages gives me more freedom to shape its structure and appearance.

We were asked to download Visual Studio Code (VS Code), the software we would use to create and edit our HTML and CSS files. I downloaded it for the workshop. We were also given a reading task: to complete a free Codecademy course, including at least the sections “Site Structure” and “A Closer Look at CSS.”

Our task in the workshop was to create a website. I was grateful to the tutors who came around the room to offer guidance and answer our questions. Their support helped when I got stuck. At the same time, I realised how important preparation is. Without previewing the material or having experience with the tools, it was difficult to follow every step at the same pace as the tutor.

My friend and I tried to keep up while also working through the language barrier—a challenge we are still working on, haha. In the end, we managed to create our websites. The tutor made the workshop lively, and the explanations were engaging and memorable. When we finished, the class applauded, which made the achievement feel even more encouraging. I really enjoyed the atmosphere.

After class, my friend and I went to a café. We discussed possible next steps for each other’s websites and previewed the material for the next class. I left feeling settled and looking forward to the next workshop.

Weekly reflection

This workshop showed me that practical learning depends on preparation as well as participation. The tutors’ guidance was helpful, but I found it difficult to follow the process when I had not yet become familiar with the material or the tools. Preparing in advance will help me take part with greater confidence.

Choosing GitHub Pages also feels like an early design decision for my portfolio. I am not only learning how to make a website; I am beginning to decide how my work and ideas will take shape online. Working alongside my friend made the learning process feel less daunting, especially while we were both navigating the language challenge.

Next time, I want to prepare before class so I can follow the technical steps more comfortably and spend more time experimenting with my own ideas.
