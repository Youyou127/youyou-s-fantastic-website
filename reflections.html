PAUSING TO NOTICE · LEEDS

Weekly Reflections.

Looking back at what challenged me, what supported me and what I want to carry into the next stage.

WEEK 01ADAPTING

Learning to Find My Bearings

My first week was an adjustment in more ways than one. Moving from China to Leeds meant learning a different rhythm of university life while also beginning a new subject. Lectures and welcome meetings gave me information, but they also made me aware of how much I was still observing and trying to understand.

I did not make anything in a workshop yet, and that made me curious about the practical work to come. Exploring Welcome Week and hearing about societies and Give It a Go activities reminded me that learning happens outside class too. Small chances to try something unfamiliar can make a new place feel more approachable.

I want to give myself time to settle in while staying open to new experiences. My first step is to keep asking questions, explore the opportunities around me and arrive at the first workshop ready to learn.

TAKE FORWARD

Curiosity can be a useful guide when everything feels new.

WEEK 02PREPARATION

Preparation Makes Space for Learning

The first website workshop showed me that practical learning moves quickly. The tutors were supportive and made time to answer questions, but I found it difficult to keep up when I had not prepared and had no previous experience with the tools. This helped me see preparation as part of the learning process, rather than an extra task.

Working alongside my friend helped us face the language challenge together. We still have to work at it, but we were able to follow the instructions, ask for help and eventually create our websites. The tutor’s lively way of teaching and the applause at the end made the room feel encouraging rather than intimidating.

Afterwards, talking through ideas in a café helped me process the session. I left with a clearer sense of what I could develop next and a practical goal: preview the material before class so I can spend more time understanding and experimenting during the workshop.

NEXT TIME

Arrive prepared, ask questions early and leave room to experiment.

WEEK 03DATA & SELECTION

What Counts as the Right Data?

This week’s web-scraping workshop made me realise that collecting data depends on the choices I make before and during the process. When I used the Web Scraper extension on BBC iPlayer, I collected less information than I expected because I had selected the wrong elements on the page. The tutor’s feedback helped me understand that a tool can only collect what I have correctly identified.

I also tried the Python approach recommended by the tutor through Real Python’s practical introduction. It was more difficult for me, but it seemed more precise because I could define the information I wanted in greater detail. I would like to keep practising so that the technical challenge becomes less of a barrier.

The workshop also left me with a broader question: what is missing when a website is turned into a dataset? Programme titles and descriptions may show what the platform makes visible, but they do not explain how people interpret the programmes or what kinds of content are absent. Scraping can be useful, but the data needs human interpretation and a clear research question.

Next time, I want to decide what I am trying to find out before choosing selectors. That should help me make more deliberate choices and understand the limits of the dataset I collect.

TAKE FORWARD

Start with a question. Then decide what to count—and what might remain unseen.

WEEK 04ETHICS & CLASSIFICATION

Categories Are Decisions

Our group’s question—how generative AI helps international students overcome language barriers—felt close to my own experience, which made the planning both interesting and personal. The brainstorm helped us turn a broad topic into possible things to observe, such as AI use for translation, reading, writing and speaking.

I am beginning to understand that a variable is not just a convenient label. If we classify a post as “helpful”, “neutral” or “not helpful”, we need to decide what counts as helpful and whose interpretation is being recorded. A tidy spreadsheet can make uncertain experiences look more definite than they really are.

We also noted that social media may not represent all international students and that privacy matters when working with ordinary users’ posts. This changed how I think about data collection: the question is not only whether material is available, but whether it is appropriate to use and what voices the chosen source leaves out.

For the next stage, I want to keep track of how our search and classification choices develop. If we cannot find enough comparable material, that difficulty itself is worth reflecting on rather than hiding.

TAKE FORWARD

Every category carries an assumption. Make the assumption visible.

WEEK 05SEARCH & REPRESENTATION

Absence Is Not the Same as No Experience

Our group found it difficult to locate enough relevant material about international students within a comparable timeframe. We looked at secondary sources, but the information we found was limited and not always reliable. This was frustrating, but it also made the methodological problem real: online material is not automatically complete, consistent or representative.

I shared the reflection that our search needed adjustment. We could refine the keywords to find more specific content while widening the range of platforms and sources beyond Reddit. These two moves work together: a focused query can help us locate relevant material, while a broader source range can reduce dependence on one community or platform.

I now see searching as part of the research method, not a neutral step before the “real” analysis. The terms we choose affect which experiences become findable. If a post is difficult to locate, it does not prove that the experience is absent; it may simply be expressed in a different vocabulary or shared in a space we have not considered.

This also affects visualisation. A chart can make a small or uneven collection look authoritative. When I add the dataset and analysis, I want to explain how the sources were found, what the categories mean and what the material cannot tell us. That context should sit beside any visual pattern, not disappear behind it.
